Understanding Real Estate Professional Status (REPS)

What is the 750-Hour Rule?

To qualify for Real Estate Professional Status under IRS rules, you must spend more than 750 hours per year in real property trades or businesses. Additionally, this time must represent more than 50% of your total personal services performed during the year. Meeting these requirements allows you to deduct rental property losses against your ordinary income, potentially saving thousands in taxes.

What Counts as Material Participation?

Material participation means you're involved in your rental property operations on a regular, continuous, and substantial basis. Qualifying activities include:

  • Property management and tenant communications
  • Maintenance, repairs, and property improvements
  • Property showings and lease negotiations
  • Rent collection and bookkeeping
  • Market research and property acquisition

The STR Loophole for High-Income Earners

Short-term rentals (STRs) with an average rental period of 7 days or less can qualify for material participation with just 100 hours per year — without needing full REPS status. This "STR loophole" is perfect for high-income W-2 earners who can't meet the 750-hour requirement but still want to deduct rental losses.

Why Documentation Matters

The IRS can audit your REPS or material participation claims up to 3 years after filing (6 years in some cases). Without detailed, contemporaneous records, you risk losing your tax benefits and facing penalties. RE Participation App creates the audit trail you need with timestamped logs, photo attachments, and comprehensive CSV exports.
